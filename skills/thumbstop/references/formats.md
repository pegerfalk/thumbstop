# Formats

Six shapes for a talking vertical video. A format fixes the **structure**
(beats, length, ending); the brief fills it. Lengths are craft defaults: TikTok's
only official figure is 21–34 s for In-Feed ads (2022), and Instagram stops
recommending Reels over 3 min to new audiences (both verified 2026-09-23,
`platforms.md`).

"Words" is the spoken budget with `--voice` (`af_heart` @ 1.1, processed:
3.4 words/s × 0.95). Without a voice, count text beats instead: about one
beat per 2–3 s.

| Format | Length | Words (voiced) | Ending | Best hook types |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| explainer | 25–45 s | 80–145 | soft CTA | `question`, `curiosity-gap`, `visual-break` |
| process | 25–45 s | 80–145 | loop to the finished result | `before-after`, `visual-break`, `number` |
| story | 30–60 s | 95–195 | soft CTA | `bold-claim`, `curiosity-gap` |
| listicle | 20–35 s | 65–115 | loop or CTA | `number`, `bold-claim` |
| objection | 20–35 s | 65–115 | soft CTA | `doing-it-wrong`, `question` |
| showcase | 15–25 s | 50–80 | loop | `visual-break`, `before-after` |

Pick the shortest length that lands the payoff. "Never a still frame" (an
event about every second beat) applies to all six. The project profile may list example briefs per format.

---

## explainer — "what is this?"

For a viewer who has never seen the thing. Teaches one concept by showing it.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 s | The thing itself, doing its most surprising part, plus the question the viewer now has |
| Name it | ~10 % | One sentence: what it is, in the viewer's words, not the trade's |
| Show it | ~45 % | The concept demonstrated on real material, one visible step per sentence |
| Why it matters | ~20 % | The one benefit, concretely (what changes for the viewer, visibly) |
| Payoff + CTA | ~15 % | The finished result held; one soft next step |

Example brief: *"What does a cold-brew concentrate actually save you?"*: the
jar, one pour, the mug, the maths of one jar against a week of café visits.

## process — "watch it get made"

Satisfying transformation. The words name the intent and the picture shows
the steps. Works muted too.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 s | The **end** result for one second (flash-forward), or the raw start with "watch this become…" |
| Steps | ~70 % | 3–5 steps, one per sentence, each a visible action; step label on screen |
| Result | ~20 % | The finished thing, held, with the sheen/reveal on it |
| Loop | tail | The last frame matches the first so the replay reads as continuous |

Example brief: *"From raw file to printed poster in 30 seconds"*: export,
print, trim, frame. Real material: screen capture of the export plus footage
of the print.

## story — "why this exists"

A person, a problem, a turn. The only format where the maker may be the
subject. Slower; earns it with a curiosity gap.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 s | The turn or the strangest true detail first |
| Setup | ~20 % | The situation before, in one or two concrete images |
| Turn | ~30 % | What changed, and the moment it did |
| Result | ~30 % | What exists now, shown |
| Close | ~15 % | One line that ties back to the hook; soft CTA |

Every detail must be true and sourced. No invented anecdotes, no composite
customers.

## listicle — "three things"

Fast, countable, rewatchable. Each item is a mini-reveal.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 s | The number and the category, with item 1 already on screen |
| Items | ~80 % | 3–5 items, equal rhythm, each: name on screen → reveal → one line of why |
| Close | ~10 % | The best item returns, or a loop into item 1 |

Put the strongest item last or first, never in the middle. Counting on
screen ("1/3") helps retention; the words do not repeat the number.

## objection — "but why not just…?"

Answers the one doubt that stops a sale. Confident, never defensive, no
hedges.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 s | The objection, stated fairly, as the viewer would say it |
| Grant | ~15 % | One sentence that takes it seriously |
| Answer | ~50 % | The concrete difference, shown on real material, one proof per sentence |
| Proof | ~20 % | A sourced number, a real result, or a side-by-side |
| Close | ~15 % | The reframe in one line; soft CTA |

Never mention competitors by name. Never claim more than the sources say.

## showcase — "just look"

Shortest format; the product carries it. The words are sparse: two to four
short lines around a real clip.

| Beat | Share | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–2 s | The most striking frame of the material, one line |
| Look | ~70 % | The real clip plays; one detail named at a time, with room between |
| Loop | tail | The end frame hands back to the start |

A bare product loop does this job on its own. A showcase earns its place
only when its words add something the loop can't: the name, the detail to
notice, the price.

---

## Endings

- **Loop:** the last frame matches or leads straight into the first frame; the
  last line also works as the opening's setup. Maximises
  replays. Default for process, listicle, showcase.
- **Soft CTA:** one line, one action, nothing else (e.g. *"The shop is in the
  profile."*). The last line is never "link in bio". Default for explainer,
  story, objection.

Never both. Never a logo sting longer than 1 s.

**Proof close** (optional, before either ending in explainer, process and
showcase): one beat of shop-level proof: the total and its reach, stacked
short (`1,200+ mugs sold` / `in 30 countries`), then the price or the CTA.
The picture pulls back from the one product to the whole range, so the number
reads as the shop's. Numbers from the profile's proof numbers, re-queried and
rounded down with a "+".
